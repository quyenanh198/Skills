# Webnovel Writer Skill

## Overview

This skill transforms webnovel planning documents into complete, publishable chapters. It reads:
- **Story outline** (acts, arcs, chapter summaries)
- **World-building** (magic system, politics, geography, etc.)
- **Character profiles** (background, motivation, arc, relationships)
- **Chapter details** (hook, events, cliffhanger, POV)

And produces:
- **Complete chapters** (3,000–5,000 words each)
- **Markdown formatted** (easy to edit, publish, or convert)
- **Batch processing** (write 5, 10, or 20 chapters in one request)
- **Zero filler** (every scene serves pacing, plot, or character)

---

## Part 1: Input Requirements

For optimal results, provide these planning documents (ideally from webnovel-planning skill):

### 1. Story Outline
- **Acts & Arcs:** Which act/arc does this chapter belong to?
- **Thematic focus:** What's the emotional/thematic weight of this arc?
- **Turning points:** Major plot beats in this arc?

### 2. World-Building
- **Magic/Power system:** How does power work? Limitations?
- **Political structure:** Who controls what? Any conflicts?
- **Geography:** Settings, cities, landmarks?
- **Social customs:** What's normal behavior? What's taboo?
- **Technology level:** Medieval? Modern? Futuristic?

### 3. Character Profiles
- **Protagonist:** Background, motivation, fear, arc
- **Supporting cast:** Key allies, rivals, love interests
- **Antagonist:** Motivation, fear, relationship to protagonist
- **Relationships:** How do characters interact? Any tension?

### 4. Chapter Details
For each chapter you want written, provide:
- **Chapter number & title**
- **POV character** (whose perspective?)
- **Opening hook** (grab reader within first 100 words)
- **Key events** (3–5 major plot points)
- **Character development** (how does protagonist change?)
- **New information** (world-building, plot, character revelation)
- **Closing cliffhanger** (unresolved tension)
- **Estimated word count** (typically 3,500 words)

## Part 2: Writing Principles

### The 15-70-15 Pacing Rule

Every chapter follows this structure:

**Hook (15%, ~500 words):**
- Immediate action, dialogue, or emotional intensity
- NO long scene-setting or world-building exposition
- Grab reader within first 100 words
- Connect directly to previous chapter's cliffhanger

**Meat (70%, ~2,500 words):**
- Protagonist or POV character faces a challenge
- New information is revealed (world, plot, character, relationship)
- Clear progression: character learns, decides, or experiences something
- Tension escalates; stakes become clearer
- Dialogue drives plot and reveals character
- Action shows emotion (don't tell reader "he was angry"—show it)

**Cliffhanger (15%, ~500 words):**
- End with unresolved tension
- Raise a question the reader must answer by reading next chapter
- Examples:
  - Unexpected threat appears
  - Hidden truth is revealed (raising new questions)
  - Character makes a shocking decision
  - Emotional twist
  - NPC reveals crucial information then vanishes
- **Never** end with resolution or answer to a major question

### Show, Don't Tell

❌ "Protagonist was terrified of the cultivator's power."
✅ "Protagonist's hands trembled as he watched the elder's aura crack the stone floor with a glance."

❌ "Lin Yao felt sad about the betrayal."
✅ "Lin Yao stared at the letter, her jaw clenched, the paper crumpling in her white-knuckled grip."

### Dialogue Best Practices

- **Every line of dialogue serves a purpose**: Move plot or reveal character
- **Cut filler**: No "how are you?" exchanges unless they're awkward/tense
- **Distinct voices**: Each character should sound different (vocabulary, pacing, topics)
- **Subtext**: What's NOT said often matters more than what is
- **Tags are minimal**: Use "she said" / "he asked"—avoid fancy tags like "she opined"

### World-Building Integration

- **Show rules through action**: Don't explain cultivation system—show character struggling with it
- **Reveal gradually**: Reader learns world as protagonist does
- **Use setting details**: Describe what matters (spiritual energy in the air, sect hierarchy via clothing)
- **Avoid info-dumps**: Never have NPC explain 3 paragraphs of lore to protagonist
- **Plant clues**: Subtle hints about world mysteries that pay off later

### Character Consistency

- Character decisions must align with their profile (motivation, fear, background)
- If character acts out of character, it should be deliberate plot point (revealed later)
- Dialogue/thought patterns should match their voice
- Relationships evolve based on events, not author convenience

## Part 3: Chapter Writing Workflow

### Step 1: Absorb Context

Before writing, understand:
1. **Story position**: What just happened? Where are we in the arc?
2. **Character state**: What does protagonist want? Fear? Believe?
3. **World state**: What's the status quo? What's been revealed?
4. **Tone**: Is this arc comedic, dark, romantic, action-packed?

### Step 2: Plan the Chapter (Internal)

Mentally map:
- **Hook scene**: What's the immediate opening? (Action? Dialogue? Reaction to previous cliffhanger?)
- **Scene sequence**: What scenes unfold in the meat? (3–5 key events from chapter outline)
- **Cliffhanger setup**: What tension emerges by chapter end?

### Step 3: Write the Chapter

- **First 100 words**: Establish immediate tension/action
- **Scenes**: 3–5 distinct scenes, each moving plot forward
- **Dialogue**: Natural, efficient, revealing
- **Description**: Minimal but vivid (focus on what matters)
- **Pacing**: Action scenes faster, emotional moments slower
- **Word count**: Hit 3,000–5,000 target (can vary ±500)

### Step 4: Self-Review Checklist

Before finalizing, verify:
- [ ] Opening hook is immediate (action/dialogue/emotion in first paragraph)
- [ ] No info-dump (world-building woven naturally into action/dialogue)
- [ ] Protagonist or POV character makes a choice or learns something
- [ ] At least one piece of new information revealed (world/plot/character/relationship)
- [ ] Dialogue is distinct and serves plot/character
- [ ] Cliffhanger is strong (question, threat, twist, decision)
- [ ] Pacing matches chapter type (action scenes faster, emotional moments slower)
- [ ] Character behavior aligns with profile and arc
- [ ] Word count is 3,000–5,000 (flexible for pacing)

## Part 4: Batch Chapter Processing

When writing multiple chapters:

1. **Maintain continuity**: Each chapter builds on previous (cliffhanger → hook)
2. **Track progression**: Ensure story momentum escalates across chapters
3. **Character consistency**: Same character behaves consistently across batch
4. **World consistency**: Established rules remain consistent
5. **Pacing variation**: Not every chapter has same intensity (some quieter for character moments, some explosive for action)

### Batch Organization

Output format for batch chapters:

```markdown
# Chapter 1: [Title]

[Chapter content]

---

# Chapter 2: [Title]

[Chapter content]

---

# Chapter 3: [Title]

[Chapter content]

...
```

Each chapter is separated by `---` for easy parsing/editing.

---

## Part 5: Output Format

All chapters output as **markdown files** with:

### File Structure

```markdown
# Chapter [#]: [Title]

[Opening hook paragraph]

[Narrative prose divided into natural scene breaks]

[Each scene flows into next without section headers]

[Dialogue integrated throughout]

[Closing paragraph ends with cliffhanger]
```

### Markdown Formatting

- **Chapter titles**: `# Chapter [#]: [Title]`
- **Scene breaks**: Blank line (no section headers for scenes)
- **Emphasis**: *Italics* for thoughts, emphasis, **bold** for rare emphasis
- **Dialogue**: Standard dialogue formatting (no special markdown needed)
- **No headers within chapters**: Let prose flow naturally

### Example Structure

```markdown
# Chapter 5: The Anomaly

Physician Wu set down her brush, her expression grave. Protagonist waited, heart pounding.

"Your meridians," she began slowly, "should not exist."

Protagonist felt ice in his chest. [Scene continues...]

---

# Chapter 6: First Cultivation Session

The morning meditation chamber was empty except for the cold-eyed senior disciple who would teach him.

"Sit," the disciple commanded. [Scene continues...]
```

## Part 6: Special Instructions by Genre

### For Xuyên Không (Time Travel/Transmigration)

- Emphasize culture clash: Modern knowledge vs. cultivation world rules
- Show protagonist's learning curve: Not instantly competent
- Play with fish-out-of-water humor when appropriate
- Revelation of "why he was transported" should come slowly
- Integration into new world should feel earned, not automatic

### For Võ Hiệp (Martial Arts/Cultivation)

- Martial arts terminology: Use Vietnamese/Chinese terms naturally (explain once, then use)
- Power progression: Show cultivation advancement through abilities, not just words
- Sect politics: Display hierarchy through action (who bows to whom, who has authority)
- Combat scenes: Focus on technique and character, not just choreography
- Honor/shame: These concepts drive character decisions

### For Cyberpunk/Sci-Fi

- Technology integration: Show tech as tool, not exposition
- World-building via environment: Futuristic details through description of setting
- Social stratification: Display class/faction differences through interaction
- Jargon: Use naturally, explain only when necessary
- Atmosphere: Cyberpunk is mood—visual details matter

## Part 7: Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### ❌ Opening with Weather

❌ "The morning sun filtered through the palace windows..."
✅ "Lin Yao's blade sang through the air, barely missing Protagonist's face."

### ❌ Over-Describing

❌ Long paragraph about the sect's architecture
✅ "The Jade Peak Sect loomed above, its towers sharp as judgmental fingers."

### ❌ Stopping for Exposition

❌ "[NPC explains cultivation system for 2 paragraphs]"
✅ "Protagonist struggled to control his spiritual energy—it flowed like water, refusing the discipline the manual demanded."

### ❌ Characters Telling Instead of Showing

❌ "Huang Ming is arrogant."
✅ "Huang Ming yawned during Protagonist's explanation, examining his nails."

### ❌ Dialogue That Doesn't Move Plot

❌ "How was your day?" "Fine. How was yours?" "Also fine."
✅ "[Cut the small talk—jump to conflict or revelation]"

### ❌ Cliffhanger That's Actually Resolved

❌ "[End of chapter]: Protagonist wonders if he can trust Lin Yao. / [Next chapter opening]: Lin Yao confesses her loyalty completely."
✅ "[End of chapter]: Protagonist wonders if he can trust Lin Yao. / [Next chapter opening]: Lin Yao's hand moves toward her blade."

## Part 8: Quality Checklist

### Story-Level Checks

- [ ] Does this chapter advance the act/arc it belongs to?
- [ ] Is protagonist closer to their Act III goal than at Act I start?
- [ ] Does this chapter's cliffhanger connect to next chapter's hook?
- [ ] Are there NO plot holes introduced without setup?

### Character-Level Checks

- [ ] Do protagonist's decisions align with their motivation/fear?
- [ ] Does at least one supporting character reveal something new?
- [ ] Do relationships show evolution (growing trust, building resentment, etc.)?
- [ ] Is protagonist's arc visible (learning, changing, struggling)?

### World-Level Checks

- [ ] Is world-building consistent with planning documents?
- [ ] Are magic/power system rules followed (not violated for convenience)?
- [ ] Do social customs/hierarchies show through action?
- [ ] Is geography/setting vivid but not info-dump?

### Writing-Level Checks

- [ ] Is pacing appropriate (faster for action, slower for emotion)?
- [ ] Is dialogue natural and distinct per character?
- [ ] Are descriptions vivid but minimal?
- [ ] Is there zero info-dump or exposition?

## Output Examples

### Example Hook (First 500 words of chapter)

```markdown
# Chapter 1: The Descent

Protagonist was falling.

That was the only coherent thought his brain could produce as the world spiraled around him—blue-white void expanding in all directions, no ground, no sky, only the endless sensation of dropping through something that might be space or might be nothing at all.

His lungs burned. He gasped for air and found only cold nothingness.

Three seconds ago (or was it three hours?) he'd been in his office in Seoul, staring at lines of code that refused to compile. His coffee was getting cold. His boss was demanding the deployment push back on schedule. Everything was normal, predictable, irritating in the way modern life tends to be.

Then the world had... cracked.

Not metaphorically. The air itself had split open like fabric tearing, and through the gap poured light so bright it burned through his closed eyelids. He'd had maybe half a second to register what was happening before the force of it hit him like a truck, and he was falling, falling into this void with nothing to hold onto and no way to stop.

His arm smashed against something solid.

The pain shocked him back into hyperawareness. Not falling through void—falling through a forest canopy. Branches tore at his clothes and skin. A branch the size of a tree trunk caught his ribs and he felt something crack (not break, please not break). He clawed for something to grab but his hands found only leaves and empty air.

Then the ground rushed up.

He hit hard enough to see stars, hard enough to drive the air from his lungs. For a long moment he just lay there in the dirt and moss, gasping like a landed fish, trying to remember how ribs worked, trying to remember what breathing felt like.

The forest was silent.

That was the first thing to penetrate his post-impact fog—the wrongness of the silence. No car horns from the city outside his office building. No sound of Seoul's endless traffic hum. No air conditioning humming from the HVAC system. Just... silence. And the smell of earth and growing things, so thick and real it seemed impossible.

Protagonist forced himself to move. Ribs screamed. His left shoulder wasn't working right. But he was alive, which was objectively more than he'd expected five minutes ago.

He got to his knees.

That's when he heard voices.

No—not voices. Footsteps, light and fast, moving through the forest with a precision that suggested whoever owned them knew exactly where they were going. Protagonist's first instinct was relief (other humans! help!), which lasted exactly as long as it took for him to see the blade.

The blade was glowing.

Just slightly—a faint blue luminescence that traced the curve of a sword being carried by a young woman in robes that looked like they'd stepped out of a historical drama. She was moving through the trees like a ghost, silent and fast, and she hadn't seen him yet.

Behind her, the air shimmered.

Protagonist watched, frozen in terror, as something massive and wrong emerged from the forest shadows. It looked like a leopard made of mist—he could see the trees through its body, but the shape was unmistakable: predator, hungry, focused on the woman with the glowing blade.

The woman spun, her blade coming up—

And Protagonist's scattered thoughts crystallized into one pure, animal certainty: he was absolutely, completely, irrevocably not in Kansas anymore.
```

### Example Cliffhanger (Final 300 words of chapter)

```markdown
The blade stopped inches from his face.

For a moment, neither of them moved. Protagonist could see the cultivator's eyes now—dark, sharp, filled with confusion and something he couldn't quite identify.

"You're not from any sect I know," she said slowly. Not a question. An observation.

Protagonist's throat was too dry to answer.

"And the beast didn't attack you." She stepped closer, her blade still raised but no longer pointed at his throat. "The spirit beasts here don't tolerate strangers. They hunt on sight."

Protagonist managed to shake his head—not a lie, just honest confusion. He had no idea what a spirit beast was. He had no idea where he was or what was happening or if he'd suffered a psychotic break and was actually lying bleeding in an alley outside his office building.

"You're injured." The cultivator's gaze swept over him, cataloging the damage. Ripped clothes, bleeding cuts, bruised ribs. "And you smell like... I don't know what you smell like."

She was still pointing a weapon at him. Protagonist decided this was not the time to correct her on the smell.

The cultivator's eyes narrowed. "Can you walk?"

Protagonist nodded carefully.

"Follow me. And stay quiet." She lowered her blade slightly. "If the sect patrol finds you wandering alone, they'll assume you're a spy. You don't want to be anywhere near a sect interrogation."

She turned and began moving through the forest, not waiting to see if he'd follow.

Protagonist, finding himself with no better options and an absolutely irrational faith that a woman with a glowing blade was still better than being alone in a forest full of spirit beasts, followed.

He had no way of knowing it, but that single decision—to follow a stranger through an impossible forest—would set the course of his entire new life.

The cultivator glanced back once, her expression unreadable, and gestured sharply for him to hurry.

Behind them, the forest shadows seemed to deepen, as if something vast and patient was watching the two tiny humans cross through its domain.

Waiting.
```
